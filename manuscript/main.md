## Introduction

Genome-scale CRISPR loss-of-function screens across cancer cell lines, such as the Cancer Dependency Map (DepMap), measure the fitness effect of knocking out each gene in each line [Tsherniak 2017; Meyers 2017; Dempster 2021 (Chronos); Arafeh 2025]. Subunits of the same protein complex tend to have correlated fitness profiles, so co-essentiality has been used to recover complexes, pathways and the functions of uncharacterised genes [Pan 2018; Boyle 2018; Kim 2019; Wainberg 2021]. Generalised least squares (GLS) on whitened profiles, which removes the dominant line-to-line covariance, is a widely used standard for building co-essential modules [Wainberg 2021].

Two problems limit how far such results can be trusted. First, method development and evaluation often share the curated complex labels used to judge success, and pair-input predictions are vulnerable to optimistic evaluation when test pairs share proteins with training pairs [Park & Marcotte 2012]. Second, downstream complex-level analyses of cancer dependencies often start from curated complex resources (CORUM [Tsitsiridis 2023], Complex Portal [Meldal 2022]) or functional networks (STRING [Szklarczyk 2023]). This makes it hard to separate what the dependency data show from what the curation already encoded.

We address both problems with a pre-registered, two-part design. In Part 1, a label-free co-complex score developed and locked in earlier work (zavg_BEF) was evaluated once on the hu.MAP 3.0 test pairs [Fischer 2025], which had been withheld throughout its development, against Pearson, Spearman and GLS. The protocol, decision rule, operating points and calibrators were frozen and hashed before the look, and all prior contact with the test files was disclosed. In Part 2, the locked score was recomputed on a construction subset of DepMap lines and converted into a hierarchical complex map without any curated complex resource, functional network or hu.MAP prediction. Orthogonal physical (BioPlex 3.0 [Huttlin 2021]) and proteomic (CCLE proteomics [Nusinow 2020]) evidence, read only after the map was frozen, tiered the map. A pre-registered "bridge" endpoint compared it with a GLS map of equal coverage. The map is the substrate for complex-level, context-specific dependency analyses: lineage, driver and genome-state contexts, within-complex genetic interactions and sub-module divergence. Their discoveries were read against registered calibrations and then confirmed in a single read of a held-out lockbox of cell lines.

The pair-level advantage of the locked score is real, but neither registered bridge to complexes held. Most complex-level context associations are shared by matched random gene sets, and they confirm at about the rate the lockbox's power predicts. We also report a defect in the confirmation code that was found after the read, how it was handled, and the registered strongest findings that remain.

### A curation-free complex map, and a null bridge

### Within-complex genetic interactions recover known biology, and most losses are neutral

H1 asks whether genetic loss of a gene changes dependency on the rest of its complex. A paralogue in the complex may become more essential (paralog compensation), the other members may become more or less essential in their baseline direction (residual sensitisation or collapse), or nothing may change beyond ±0.1 Chronos units (neutral, by equivalence testing with two one-sided tests [Schuirmann 1987]). Each class also requires the effect to lie outside a matched null of random member sets. Two amendments made before any H1 association was run, from event counts and types only, define the panel: A1 moved 318 expression-silencing events to the exploratory entry H1S, and A2 defined genetic loss as LoF or homozygous deletion in at least half of an event's loss lines. <!-- docs/registry/AMENDMENTS.md A1; docs/registry/H1_within_complex_GI.yaml --> The panel has 24 events and 26 tests; 24 were evaluable (registered m = 28), and 5 were discoveries. <!-- DR/H1_within_complex_GI/summary.json: n_events, n_tests, trial_line.n_evaluable, trial_line.n_units, n_discovery -->

### The lockbox read, and a defect in the confirmation code

A dry check stopped the confirmation step before any lockbox access, because four registered steps had no code. They were implemented as registered. ### About half the associations confirm, as the lockbox's power predicts

Of 1,145 H3a discoveries, 601 were confirmed in L (52.5%; 524 of 942 non-locus). <!-- CMP rows H3a_lineage_atlas: primary_n_lockbox, primary_n_confirmed, primary_rate_evaluable --> Confirmation differed by lineage: 137 of 197 in Lymphoid, against 6 of 30 in Esophagus/Stomach, the lineage that was also fragile under the screen-quality sensitivity. <!-- LD: H3a.confirmed_per_context --> The H3b rate is **0.49 among evaluable fits (60 of 122), 0.40 counting all fits (60 of 150) and 0.46 in the corrected relook (69 of 150)** (Fig. 5a; Supplementary Table S6). <!-- CMP rows H3b_driver_family overall: primary_rate_evaluable, primary_rate_all_in, corrected_rate_evaluable, primary_n_confirmed, primary_n_lockbox, corrected_n_confirmed; calc: 150-28 --> Because the NOT EVALUABLE fits are concentrated in WGD, the evaluable rate is biased upward, and none of the three should be read alone. ## Discussion

This study tested one label-free co-essentiality score through two registered stages: a single look at withheld co-complex pairs, and a single read of held-out cell lines for every complex-level claim built on it. The outcome is mixed.

**The method wins the test it was built for.** On held-out hu.MAP 3.0 complexes, zavg_BEF beat GLS, Spearman and Pearson under a decision rule frozen before the look, and the gain over GLS held on protein-new pairs. Two qualifications define its scope. The gain is carried by complexes with many positive pairs, and for clusters with five or fewer positive pairs the locked score is not better. The method was also selected on hu.MAP training labels derived from Complex Portal [Fischer 2025], so agreement with curated complexes partly re-measures that selection.

**Its advantage does not carry over to complexes.** Both registered bridges failed. The BioPlex bridge was null (−0.025, −0.075 to +0.031), and protein co-abundance favoured GLS. BRIDGE-L was negative: discoveries on the zavg map confirmed less often than those on the GLS map (−0.065, −0.107 to −0.025), also at matched coverage and among physically supported clusters. <!-- BR: primary.diff, primary.ci95; BLR7: primary_evaluable.primary.diff, primary_evaluable.primary.ci95 --> The post hoc locus explanation of the first bridge does not account for the second. On present evidence, the zavg map's value lies in its larger gene coverage, not in better complexes. A user who wants context-replicable modules would do at least as well with GLS modules. We do not know why the better pair-level score yields no better map. One untested possibility is that any reasonable clustering already captures the gain on pair-rich complexes, while the zavg map's extra coverage adds weaker modules.

**Most complex-level context associations are not about complexes.** Matched random gene sets associated with lineages and drivers as often as real complexes, and even never-expressed gene sets showed a lineage excess. A complex-level dependency atlas, ours included, mostly records gene-set-generic effects: shifts of the Chronos scale, screen-quality differences and broad changes in essential-gene dependency. Earlier module-level lineage analyses did not calibrate against such matched sets [Wainberg 2021; Chen 2023], so we cannot say how much of their signal is complex-specific. Our results suggest that the question needs this calibration.

**About half the associations replicate, which is what power predicts.** In 338 held-out lines, 52.5% of lineage and 40–49% of driver associations were confirmed, close to the 47–52% and 43–44% that a post hoc power aid predicts once the A effects are shrunk. <!-- MAN: n_L; CMP rows overall: primary_rate_evaluable, primary_rate_all_in; LD: power_band --> Complex-specific discoveries confirmed more often, but by about the margin power predicts. Complex scores did not replicate better than their best single gene, and where few members carry the association, the single gene replicated clearly better. For most context dependencies, the complex is not the right unit.

**What survives.** The 254 registered strongest findings pass every registered check: they were confirmed in held-out lines, are robust to leaving out each primary disease or lineage on A, lie outside locus clusters and do not depend on the code defect. We lead with T1 findings whose effects depart from their lineage's essentiality trend, such as ovarian dependency on a CDH1/PAX8 junction unit, bone dependency on SNAPC, and Myeloid dependency on AP2. We set aside 56 lymphoid and myeloid findings whose effects the lineage-scale trend could explain. <!-- ROB: per_direction.*.strongest; SD: n, possible_lineage_scale_artefact --> These are replicated, robustness-checked associations at the level of a module. Neither the matched-set comparison nor the lockbox shows that the complex, rather than one or two members, is the operative unit, and none has been tested outside DepMap.

**Within complexes, the common outcome is no change.** The H1 panel recovered known paralog compensation and partner dependency, all of which is registered control biology or published: ARID1A → ARID1B [Helming 2014], EP300 → CREBBP [Ogiwara 2016] and FOCAD → PELO–HBS1L [Prindle 2025]. The informative result is the neutral one. Eleven of 24 genetic losses, most of them tumour suppressors, left their complex's dependency unchanged within ±0.1 Chronos units. <!-- PD: H1.n_neutral, H1.n_events; docs/registry/H1_within_complex_GI.yaml --> Where something changes, it is a single paralogue or partner, which agrees with the view that paralog buffering acts within complexes [De Kegel & Ryan 2019].

**Amendments and post hoc decisions.** Table 3 lists every change after the design freeze with its timing relative to access to outcomes; the full record is Supplementary Note 14. The calibrations that changed our reading most were registered but implemented only after the discovery runs. The defect in the lockbox driver was found only after the read, although the rehearsal had shown its failures. Keeping the read as executed as the primary result and stripping claims from every call that the correction changes was the conservative choice. It costs the WGD context and four controls their lockbox evidence. The registered robustness checks were run after the read, on A only.

**Limitations.** All dependencies come from one CRISPR resource, and the rare-context route is not an independent replication. The lockbox is small (338 lines), so non-confirmation of small effects is often uninformative, and L cannot re-establish equivalence for the neutral H1 events. "Complex" means a co-dependency module with physical or proteomic support, not a curated complex. Several rules were fixed after outcomes were seen: E2, the defect-sensitive label, the power aid, the robustness readings and the lead and artefact labels in Table S9. All of them make the reading more conservative or are descriptive. Analyses, reviews and rulings were carried out by AI agents under a human principal investigator; independent agent reviews reproduced every confirmatory number, but reviewers and producers share a model family. The full list is in Supplementary Note 15. <!-- MAN: n_L -->

## Conclusions

A label-free co-complex score, locked before the test data existed, outperformed Pearson, Spearman and GLS on held-out hu.MAP 3.0 complexes, with the gain concentrated in pair-rich complexes. The advantage did not carry over to complexes: one registered bridge was null and the other negative. Complex-level context associations in DepMap largely reflect gene-set-generic lineage and driver effects. They confirm at the rate power predicts, and complex scores did not replicate better than their best gene. Within complexes, known paralog compensation was recovered and most genetic losses were neutral. The 254 registered strongest findings are candidates for testing outside DepMap. <!-- SD: n --> Any complex-level dependency analysis should test complex-specificity against matched gene sets rather than assume it.

### The locked method

zavg_BEF is described in the Part 1 repository, locked at commit c7f95ee and used from a read-only clone. It is the equal-weight mean of three component z-scores computed on gene-standardised CRISPRGeneEffect profiles with the top 20 principal components removed and ridge whitening: first-order correlation (F), second-order neighbourhood cosine (B) and a Laplacian-eigenmap cosine (E). <!-- LITERATURE.md §0 --> It has no parameters fitted to labels; its configuration was selected on hu.MAP training pairs by complex-grouped cross-validation, with one internal final test built from training pairs. Proteins were mapped UniProt → HGNC → Entrez → DepMap column as in Part 1. The baselines are Part 1's development-selected variants: signed Pearson and Spearman correlation and signed GLS t-statistics [Wainberg 2021], with Part 1's missing-data protocol. A pre-declared secondary candidate, the second-order component B alone, and a post hoc PC-removed Pearson reference are reported in Supplementary Note 10. Nothing learned in this study changed the method.

### Test protocol

The protocol (docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md v1.2) was revised through three design-review rounds and frozen before the look. Pairs were canonicalised as unordered UniProt pairs; self pairs and pairs in both files were excluded, and pairs also in a training file would have been kept (none existed). Unmapped pairs (33,931 of 600,738) were kept at the predictor's floor score, min(mapped scores) − 1, as one tied bottom block for every method (intention-to-score, ITT). <!-- TR: results.itt.n_unmapped_any, audit.unique_canonical_pairs; docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md --> S1 is the set of pairs mapped under every method, Part 1's evaluation universe. Strata C1–C3 contain pairs with both, one or neither protein among training-positive proteins. Only ID files were scored, so no label reached the predictor.

*Metrics.* The primary metric is non-interpolated average precision at natural prevalence, computed with Part 1's metric functions [Saito & Rehmsmeier 2015; Davis & Goadrich 2006; Boyd 2013]. Secondary metrics are AUROC; precision at fixed recall; AP with hard negatives only; macro per-cluster AP; AP without the largest component and without the 10 largest clusters; AP in essentiality strata; and precision, recall and F1 at three pre-specified rules. Rule (b), the primary operating point, maximises F1 over all hu.MAP training pairs. Rule (c) predicts the top K = round(π_train × N) pairs. Rule (a), z ≥ 3 for zavg_BEF only, is non-blind (D1). Test clusters are connected components of the test-positive graph; components larger than 40 proteins were to be split by Louvain [Blondel 2008], but none needed splitting. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §5; TR: results.itt.cluster_stats.components_split -->

*Calibration.* Each method's scores were mapped to probabilities by isotonic regression (primary) and Platt scaling (secondary), fitted on all 634,779 hu.MAP training pairs and applied unchanged [Zadrozny & Elkan 2002; Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana 2005]. Brier score and skill, calibration-in-the-large (expressed as the ratio Σp/Σy), calibration intercept and slope, ECE and reliability diagrams were reported [Van Calster 2019]. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §5.5 --> No CI was pre-specified for calibration-in-the-large, and no recalibration was made.

*Uncertainty.* The primary interval is Part 1's paired cluster bootstrap [Field & Welsh 2007]. In each replicate, clusters are drawn with replacement, a within-cluster pair is weighted by its cluster's multiplicity and a between-cluster pair by the product of multiplicities; B = 2,000, seed 0, 95% percentile intervals and two-sided p = 2·min(P(Δ ≤ 0), P(Δ ≥ 0)). Protein (node) [Owen 2007] and stratified pair bootstraps are secondary, the latter anti-conservative; Monte-Carlo seeds 1–4 show seed dependence. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §6 --> Thresholds and calibrators were not refitted within replicates, so their sampling error is not propagated.

*Decision rule.* zavg_BEF "clearly beats" the baselines if, against each of GLS, Spearman and Pearson, the relative AP gain is at least 10% (point estimate) and macro per-cluster AP and AP without the largest component are at least 0.95× the baseline's (intersection–union), computed by Part 1's unchanged function on S1. B was confirmatory only if zavg_BEF passed (fixed sequence). The CI-excludes-0 statistic, Holm-adjusted p-values [Holm 1979, M3] and non-degradation ratio CIs were pre-specified and reported but do not alter the decision. Outcome readings were pre-written. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §7 -->

*Single look and verification.* The freeze marker hashed the protocol, fitted rules, evaluation code and tests, package list and test files, and every test-touching entry point verified it. The look required a committed intent, committed a "started" record before reading labels, and refused to run if any earlier look existed on disk, in any ref or in the reflog; a re-run would have been non-confirmatory automatically. These measures are procedural: a process with file access could read the labels, so the guarantee rests on the agent briefs, the disclosure of all prior contact and externally reported hashes. One verifier re-implemented the protocol without importing the evaluation package. A second re-scored a 1% sample of pairs (seed 7 instead of the protocol's seed 0, a disclosed deviation). <!-- TV: "Deviation from §8.3" --> Before the freeze, a dry run reproduced Part 1's internal final test to ≤ 1.5 × 10⁻⁸. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §10 -->

### Firewalls, lockbox and contexts

Part 2 code may not read the hu.MAP files, test results or training labels, and no Part 2 parameter cites a test metric; tests enforce this. Construction and selection inputs are A's CRISPRGeneEffect, the locked predictor, Model, Confounders, HGNC and Ensembl coordinates. BioPlex, CCLE proteomics, DEMETER2 and the lockbox are validation inputs only. The context-table builder read CCLE proteomics for the pre-registered LoF functional check before the map was committed; this use is permitted, concerns each gene's own protein level, and no construction module imports it.

The universe U is the 1,345 CRISPRGeneEffect lines that are not engineered or drug-adapted. The lockbox L (338 lines, 25.1%) was drawn by iterative stratification [Sechidis 2011, M9] on PatientID blocks, using model labels only, with one label per lineage and 18 genotype labels; no patient straddles A and L, and A = U \ L. <!-- MAN: n_U, n_L, n_patients_straddling; results/part2/lockbox/README.md; calc: 338/1345 --> Lockbox outcomes are read only through a ledgered function, once per registry entry, after a committed target list.

Contexts were derived from DepMap Omics and Model.csv (DESIGN §2.2). They are MSI-H (MSIScore > 20); LoF (biallelic damaging, damaging plus copy loss, or homozygous deletion, subject to a pre-registered functional check of lower expression or protein; MSI-H lines excluded from non-MMR LoF contexts); hotspots; copy number on the ploidy-relative scale, merged into segments; registered fusions; and sex, loss of Y, WGD, TP53 status, NRF2 activity, lineage and melanoma. Missing values were not imputed. Contexts with at least 12 A lines are confirmatory, and rarer ones go by a labelled rare-context route. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.2 -->

### Complex construction

zavg_BEF was recomputed on A with the locked configuration, with null moments recomputed per run; on all U lines the recomputation reproduces Part 1 scores to ≤ 10⁻¹². Score blocks were computed on A, on 20 PatientID-blocked 80% subsamples and on 4 lineage-stratified split halves. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3; results/part2/construction/README.md "Pipeline" --> S contains genes with effect < −0.5 in at least 1% of A lines, non-missing in at least 95% and not low-coverage in Part 1. <!-- results/part2/construction/zavg_map/parameters.json: S -->

*Graph and clustering.* An edge joins genes with score ≥ z0 that are mutual top-k neighbours within S, with weight min(score, z0 + 3) − z0 + 1. Co-clustering frequencies over the 20 subsamples × 5 Leiden seeds were clustered with Leiden-CPM over a resolution grid [Traag 2019; Traag 2011; Fortunato & Barthélemy 2007; Monti 2003; Lancichinetti & Fortunato 2012; Jeub 2018; Zheng 2021 (HiDeF)]. A cluster is persistent when it recurs (Jaccard ≥ 0.75) over at least 3 adjacent resolutions with support ≥ 0.6. Support in a subsample is containment of at least 75% of its members in one run cluster, a rule chosen on a toy simulation before any map was built because Jaccard support lost 3–4-gene complexes. <!-- results/part2/construction/zavg_map/parameters.json: weight_cap, jaccard_recur, support_min, min_adjacent; results/part2/construction/DECISIONS.md D1 --> Candidates were placed most-persistent-first, with nested candidates kept as sub-modules and partial overlaps resolved to the more persistent cluster. Level-1 complexes have 3–60 members. Sub-modules come from top-down re-clustering inside each complex and need a mean score at least 1 above z0. On the toy simulation, these rules recovered 86 of 105 planted complexes and all 39 planted sub-modules, and returned no cluster on null data. <!-- results/part2/construction/DECISIONS.md D2, "Toy results"; results/part2/construction/zavg_map/parameters.json: cohesion_delta -->

*Selection.* For each (z0, k) in {4, 5, 6} × {10, 20}, each half of four PatientID-blocked lineage-stratified splits was clustered through the same pipeline. Non-locus half-1 clusters of size 5–30 count as recovered when a half-2 cluster matches with Jaccard ≥ 0.5, and the statistic is the selective-gene coverage of recovered clusters, averaged over both directions and the four splits. The highest mean was selected, and a degree-rewired baseline was reported. <!-- results/part2/construction/DECISIONS.md D6 -->

*GLS comparator, locus flag and attachment.* For each run, the GLS graph uses the same S and k and the same edge count as the zavg graph of that model set, with weights quantile-matched, so both maps remove the same score quantile. A cluster is a locus cluster if at least 50% of its members lie within 10 Mb or on one chromosome arm. Genes outside S are attached to a cluster when at least half its members score ≥ z0 with the gene and a hypergeometric null gives BH q < 0.01; attached genes are not members. <!-- results/part2/construction/DECISIONS.md D3, D4; DESIGN.md §2.3 -->

*Tiers.* T1 requires within-cluster BioPlex edges (293T ∪ HCT116; at least 3 observed members and 2 edges) above a null that replaces each member by a random S gene of the same log2 BioPlex degree bin and essentiality class (10⁴ draws; BH q < 0.05). T2 (not T1) requires mean pairwise protein correlation among at least 3 measured members (A lines only) above a size-matched null. T3-neg clusters were testable but not supported; T3-nc clusters were not testable. <!-- results/part2/construction/DECISIONS.md D5 -->

*Bridge and variability.* The unit is the level-1 cluster, locus clusters included. Within each size bin {3–4, 5–8, 9–16, 17–60}, BioPlex precision is pooled over within-cluster pairs with both proteins observed. Bins are averaged with weights from the pooled cluster-size distribution after coverage matching. The map with more genes drops its least stable clusters until coverage matches. The CI is a protein-node bootstrap (B = 2,000, seed 5000) with clusters fixed and each pair weighted by the product of its nodes' multiplicities. Sensitivities (no matching; locus clusters excluded) and secondary endpoints (co-abundance; stability by size; pair-level AP against BioPlex; BRIDGE-L) were fixed before validation. <!-- results/part2/construction/DECISIONS.md D5; BR: sensitivity.no_coverage_matching.B, sensitivity.no_coverage_matching.seed --> Map-to-map variability was added after the freeze as the descriptive analysis DESIGN requires, without changing the frozen maps.

### Association statistics

*Complex score.* S_C is the mean over the fixed members of C of CRISPRGeneEffect, each value clipped to [−3, 3]. For a given test, the context gene(s), their paralogues within C and members within 10 Mb of a copy-number context gene are removed, and S_C is missing if fewer than half the remaining members are observed. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml -->

*Model and test.* S_C ~ context + covariates is fitted by ordinary least squares. Library, screen type and model type enter all tests; lineage fixed effects enter non-lineage contexts; mutation burden and MSIScore enter mutation contexts; and ploidy, WGD and TP53 status enter copy-number contexts. No covariate that is a function of the context is used. Indicator levels with fewer than two lines in the analysed sample are merged into the reference. This engine convention is not stated in DESIGN and was noted by the controls review; with full lineage fixed effects instead, control p-values change by at most 2% (relative). <!-- results/part2/verify/controls_review.md --> The context coefficient is tested with HC3 standard errors [MacKinnon & White 1985, M4; Long & Ervin 2000, M5] and Satterthwaite (Bell–McCaffrey) degrees of freedom [Satterthwaite 1946, M6; Bell & McCaffrey 2002, M7]. A test is evaluable with at least 12 context and 12 reference A lines and at least 2 members after removals. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml -->

*Permutation.* Screening hits (BB-adjusted q < 0.1) are re-tested by exact PatientID-blocked Freedman–Lane permutation [Winkler 2014] with 10,000 permutations, within lineage for non-lineage contexts. The p-value is (k + 1)/(B + 1), and a generalised Pareto tail approximation [Knijnenburg 2009, M8] is used only when that value falls below 10⁻⁴. Permutation p-values re-enter the FDR procedure. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml --> An earlier implementation applied the tail approximation more broadly with 1,000 permutations and inflated the test size in simulation. It was corrected to the registered rule on synthetic data, before any real association was run. <!-- results/part2/verify/h3a_fdr_diagnosis.md "Fix" --> Tables report extrapolated values as "< 10⁻⁴ (GPD)" and rank by |t|. <!-- RESEARCH_TREE.md D8 R3 -->

*Error control.* H3a and H3b use Benjamini–Bogomolov selective FDR [Benjamini & Bogomolov 2014, M1]. Families are contexts, a family's p-value is the Simes combination over its units [Simes 1986, M2], families are selected by BH [Benjamini & Hochberg 1995] at q over the M families, and within each of the R selected families BH is applied at q·R/M. This controls the expected within-family false discovery proportion averaged over selected families. In 1,000 simulations with planted lineage effects the selective error rate was 0.046 at q = 0.05; the pooled proportion (0.068) is reported but not claimed. <!-- results/part2/verify/h3a_fdr_diagnosis.md "Validation"; docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml --> H1 uses BH across events. H2 tests sub-modules, residualised on the rest of the parent, stage-wise within discovered parents [Van den Berge 2017].

*H1.* For each genetic-loss event, the paralog axis β_P is the loss coefficient of the in-complex paralogue(s). The complex-wide axis κ_N is the loss coefficient of the mean of the non-paralog members, each aligned to its baseline direction of dependency. Classes need the effect outside a matched null of random member sets from other complexes. Neutral means a 90% CI inside ±0.1 Chronos units by two one-sided tests at α = 0.05 [Schuirmann 1987]. <!-- docs/registry/H1_within_complex_GI.yaml --> The registry lists member-resolved β_j profiles as an "also reported" item. They were exported from A after the discovery run with the engine's own per-member fit; the export reads no lockbox data, changes no call, and was committed during the lockbox read window.

*Calibrations.* One hundred pseudo-contexts per direction, matched to real contexts in size and lineage composition, ran with the discovery. The other registered calibrations had no implementation when the discovery runs were made. They were implemented and run once each on A after all discovery runs and before any lockbox read, with operational choices fixed in a committed intent first (ruling R4). These are matched random gene sets (10 per complex, drawn from members of one other complex, with essentiality within ±0.1 and coherence within ±1.0 z); 100 dispersed sets of genes not expressed in any RNA-profiled A line; a copy-number segment null; and 20 random member splits per H2 sub-module. <!-- DR/CALIBRATION_D8_INTENT.md --> The registered response, permutation re-testing of every real test with p < 0.01 if the pseudo-context fraction exceeds 0.075, did not fire. The flag "beats all matched sets" (|t| above that of each matched set in the same context) was fixed as a reporting rule before the lockbox read. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml; RESEARCH_TREE.md D8 -->

*Sensitivities.* Three sensitivities were declared before they were computed; none writes a trial line or changes a discovery. They are screen-quality covariates (ScreenNNMD and CasActivity; complete case); p = max(p_perm, analytic p) with the FDR procedure re-run; and ploidy, WGD and TP53 status as copy-number covariates for SMARCB1 (Supplementary Note 13).

### Lockbox confirmation

A discovery is confirmed if its L effect has the same sign and one-sided p < 0.05 (same score, removals, covariates and test; exact PatientID-blocked permutation when L has fewer than 20 context lines), for contexts with at least 5 context and 5 reference L lines. Rarer contexts take the labelled rare-context route: leave-one-lineage-out, DEMETER2 on A lines and leave-one-line-out must all give the same sign. L effects with 95% CIs are reported for every discovery. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml --> Lockbox permutation seeds were registered by A3. Each read was persisted to an untracked cache for crash recovery, with its SHA-256 committed at once; only the driver's resume path may reopen it, and a test enforces this. The single read used `scripts/51_lockbox_confirm.py` at code commit 7d3c23a, after the intent ed065f4, and no source file changed during the read. That the loader was called exactly 30 times is inferred from ledger invariants and file times, because the run log was not committed. <!-- RV §1 -->

*BENCH.* For each discovery in a lockbox-testable context, g* is the member with the largest A |t| in the complex's direction, chosen in A after the context's removals. Δ = t_L(complex score) − t_L(g*), signed toward the A direction. Breadth is the fraction of all members with one-sided p < 0.05 in A. The registered criterion is the CI of the high-breadth tercile, from a PatientID-blocked bootstrap over L lines with pairs and g* fixed (B = 2,000, seed 5100). <!-- docs/registry/BENCH_complex_vs_gene.yaml --> The within-line-rank multiverse score and an A–L random-set correlation were declared not run before the read (A3–A5).

*BRIDGE-L.* GLS-map discoveries were made on A with the H3a/H3b machinery before the lockbox read. Per map, the confirmation rate is computed within the size bins and averaged with the pooled bin weights of the discovered clusters (renormalised over the bins present, A5), with a bootstrap over hits within map and bin (B = 2,000, seed 5200). <!-- docs/registry/BRIDGE-L_confirmation_rate.yaml; docs/registry/AMENDMENTS.md A5 -->

*Driver defect, R6 and R7.* The L covariate design was assembled over all models and then subset, instead of over the L rows read, as on the A side. The fix (38e301a) builds it on the L rows with the engine's conventions, and a unit test reproduces the failure. The corrected relook reads only the persisted, hash-checked matrices, never the loader, and writes no ledger line; it ran after the R6 and R7 notes and an intent had been committed. Calls that differ between the read as executed and the relook are defect-sensitive (Supplementary Note 3).

*Post hoc power aid.* For each H3a and H3b hit on the lockbox route, the independent reviewer computed the power of the one-sided α = 0.05 test in L, using a noncentral t with the L degrees of freedom. The effect was taken as the empirical-Bayes posterior of the A estimate (Tweedie's formula [Efron 2011] on the z-scores of all evaluable A tests of the direction, with a degree-7 Poisson log-density fit), either as the posterior mean or integrated over the posterior variance. The SE was the A SE scaled by the L/A line counts or the observed L SE. The expected rate is the mean power over hits, and the band spans these variants. This analysis was not registered, was made after the read and is not a test (Supplementary Note 4). <!-- RV §4 -->

### Strongest findings and annotations

The registered rule (DESIGN §2.5; each entry's success criterion) requires discovery q < 0.05, primary confirmation or a passed rare-context route, no defect sensitivity, no locus flag and leave-one-out robustness: every evaluable fold has the same sign as the full A fit and p < 0.05. A fold that leaves fewer than 2 context or reference lines is skipped, not failed. The unit is OncotreePrimaryDisease for H3a and OncotreeLineage for H3b and H1, and H1 calls must also be headline-eligible under E2. Leave-top-2-out and breadth are reported per hit, never selected on. Findings are ranked by tier, then |t_A|. <!-- ROBR: "Rule (applied mechanically)" --> The rule, units and candidate list were committed before computation (fd03b03). The checks use A only, and a full refit reproduced every committed discovery β (to 10⁻⁶) and t (to 10⁻⁴). <!-- ROBR: "Reproduction check" -->

The annotations (`scripts/57_annotate_strongest.py`) are post hoc and descriptive. They are the matched-set flag; control recovery; whether leave-one-out was partly untestable; unit essentiality on A; and, for H3a, the context's least-squares trend of β on unit essentiality over all 367 tested complexes, with each hit's residual z (residual divided by the context's residual SD). <!-- ROBR: "Post hoc descriptive annotations" --> Supplementary Table S9 (`scripts/tab_strongest.py`) adds two post hoc reporting labels (D16). *Lead example*: H3a, T1, not a control readout, |residual z| ≥ 2. *Possible lineage-scale artefact*: H3a in Lymphoid or Myeloid, sign as the trend predicts, |residual z| < 2. <!-- manuscript/tables/tableS9_strongest_findings.md -->

### Registry, amendments and reproducibility

Every analysis is a committed, machine-readable registry entry; the engine refuses unregistered, modified or placeholder entries and appends every run to a trial ledger. Freeze points (test protocol, lockbox, maps, contexts and families) are commits with SHA-256 manifests that the engine verifies. Amendments, errata and rulings are recorded with reasons and changed fields in docs/registry/AMENDMENTS.md and in each touched entry. Table 3 summarises them, and Supplementary Note 14 gives the full table with times. Seeds are fixed throughout (Supplementary Note 9). Every headline number was re-derived by an agent that did not produce it: the test look; maps, tiers and bridge; contexts; controls; discovery runs; and lockbox calls, rates and BRIDGE-L. Figures and tables are generated deterministically from committed result files only (`scripts/fig_*.py`, `scripts/tab_*.py`), and a second run reproduces every figure file byte for byte. `scripts/check_manuscript_numbers.py` checks every number in the main text against the committed file named in its source comment. Compute ran on a laptop: one score run on A took 159 s, the map builds about 4 min each and the single lockbox read 2.6 h. <!-- results/part2/construction/README.md "Runtimes"; RESEARCH_TREE.md D12 -->
