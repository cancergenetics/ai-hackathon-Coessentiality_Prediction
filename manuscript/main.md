## Abstract

**Background.** Genes whose loss-of-function fitness effects co-vary across cancer cell lines (co-essentiality) often encode subunits of the same protein complex, but co-essentiality methods are usually tuned and evaluated on overlapping curated labels, and complex-level analyses of cancer dependencies commonly build on curated complex definitions.

**Methods.** The study was pre-registered in two parts. In Part 1, a label-free co-complex score (zavg_BEF), locked before the evaluation data existed, was evaluated in a single look at the withheld hu.MAP 3.0 test pairs (3,312 positive, 597,426 negative) against Pearson, Spearman and GLS. <!-- TR: audit.analysed_pos, audit.analysed_neg --> In Part 2, the score was turned into a curation-free complex map on a construction set of 1,007 DepMap lines. Complex-level associations with lineage and driver or genome-state contexts, within-complex genetic interactions and sub-module divergence were then tested against registered calibrations, and confirmed in one read of 338 held-out lockbox lines. <!-- results/part2/lockbox/manifest.json: n_A, n_L -->

**Results.** In Part 1, average precision (AP) was 0.333 (95% cluster-bootstrap CI 0.256–0.410) against 0.212 for GLS, 0.204 for Spearman and 0.197 for Pearson. The paired gain over GLS was +0.121 (+0.079 to +0.159), and the pre-registered decision rule was met against all three baselines. <!-- TR: results.itt.bootstrap.cluster.stat.*|ap; results.itt.bootstrap.cluster.diff."zavg_BEF|ap - gls_signed|ap"; results.mapped_only.locked_verdict --> The gain came from pair-rich clusters: the unweighted per-cluster average showed no gain (macro AP 0.297 vs 0.292). <!-- TR: results.itt.metrics.*.macro_cluster_ap --> The Part 2 map has 367 complexes covering 3,642 genes. <!-- results/part2/construction/summary/summary.json: zavg.n_level1, zavg.genes_level1 --> Neither pre-registered bridge from the pair-level advantage to complexes held. Agreement with BioPlex physical interactions did not differ from that of a GLS map of equal coverage (difference in size-standardised precision −0.025, −0.075 to +0.031). <!-- BR: primary.diff, primary.ci95 --> Associations discovered on the zavg map were confirmed in the lockbox less often than those discovered on the GLS map (−0.065, −0.107 to −0.025). <!-- BLR7: primary_evaluable.primary.diff, ci95 --> On the construction set, lineage and driver contexts gave 1,145 and 161 complex-level associations under selective FDR control. Random gene sets matched for size, essentiality and coherence reached p < 0.05 as often as real complexes (28.1% vs 28.1%; 11.6% vs 12.0%), so most associations reflect gene-set-generic lineage and driver effects. <!-- DR/H3a_lineage_atlas/summary.json, DR/H3b_driver_family/summary.json: n_discovery; DR/*/calibration_D8.json: matched_random_sets.pooled, real_pooled --> In the lockbox, 52.5% of lineage associations were confirmed. For driver associations the rate was 40% counting all fits, 49% among evaluable fits and 46% after a disclosed, non-confirmatory correction of a defect in the confirmation code. These rates are close to the 47–52% and 43–44% that a post hoc power calculation predicts for L. <!-- CMP: H3a overall, H3b overall; LD: power_band --> Complex scores did not replicate better than their best single member: the registered high-breadth comparison had a 95% CI that included 0. <!-- LB/BENCH_complex_vs_gene/bench.json: primary.ci95_high --> The within-complex panel recovered known paralog compensation: ARID1A loss → ARID1B was confirmed, but it is also a registered positive control, and EP300 loss → CREBBP passed only the rare-context route (4 lockbox loss lines). A registered per-hit robustness check has not yet been run on them. Examples are higher lymphoid dependency on a unit including ADSL, ATIC, CAD, CTPS1, DHODH, GART, GMPS and IMPDH2, higher bowel dependency on a unit including CTNNB1, TCF7L2, HNF1A, HNF4A, GATA6 and FOXA2, and higher dependency of RB1-deficient lines on a unit including CCNE1, CCNE2, CDK2, E2F1, E2F3 and SKP2. <!-- LD: H3a.n_strongest, H3b.n_strongest; manuscript/tables/tableS7_lockbox_h3_full.tsv rows H3a lineage:Lymphoid Z0030, lineage:Bowel Z0092; H3b LoF:RB1 Z0069 -->

**Conclusions.** A label-free co-essentiality score that wins a pre-registered held-out test of co-complex pairs does not, on this evidence, yield better complexes. At complex level, most context dependencies are shared by matched random gene sets, about half confirm in held-out lines at the rate power predicts, and complex scores did not out-replicate the best single gene. Complex-specificity has to be tested against matched gene sets, not assumed.

## Introduction

Genome-scale CRISPR loss-of-function screens across large cancer cell-line panels, such as the Cancer Dependency Map (DepMap), measure the fitness effect of knocking out each gene in each line [Tsherniak 2017; Meyers 2017; Dempster 2021 (Chronos); Arafeh 2025]. Subunits of the same protein complex tend to have correlated fitness profiles across lines, so co-essentiality has been used to recover complexes, pathways and the functions of uncharacterised genes [Pan 2018; Boyle 2018; Kim 2019; Wainberg 2021]. Generalised least squares (GLS) on whitened profiles, which removes the dominant line-to-line covariance, is a widely used standard for building co-essential modules [Wainberg 2021].

Two problems limit how far such results can be trusted. First, method development and evaluation frequently share the curated complex labels used to judge success, and pair-input predictions are vulnerable to optimistic evaluation when test pairs share proteins with training pairs [Park & Marcotte 2012]. Second, downstream complex-level analyses of cancer dependencies usually start from curated complex resources (CORUM, Complex Portal) or functional networks (STRING) [Tsitsiridis 2023; Meldal 2022; Szklarczyk 2023], which makes it hard to separate what the dependency data show from what the curation already encoded.

We address both problems with a pre-registered, two-part design. In Part 1, a label-free co-complex score developed and locked in earlier work (zavg_BEF; Part 1 repository commit c7f95ee) <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §0 "Other facts at the freeze" --> was evaluated once on the hu.MAP 3.0 test pairs [Fischer 2025], which were withheld throughout its development, against the conventional baselines used in that development (Pearson, Spearman, GLS). The protocol, decision rule, operating points and calibrators were frozen and hashed before the look, and all prior contact with the test files was disclosed. In Part 2, the locked score, recomputed on a construction subset of DepMap lines, was converted into a hierarchical complex map without any curated complex resource, functional network or hu.MAP prediction. The map was tiered by orthogonal physical (BioPlex 3.0 [Huttlin 2021]) and proteomic (CCLE proteomics [Nusinow 2020]) evidence read only after the map was frozen, and compared with a GLS map of equal coverage in a pre-registered "bridge" endpoint. The map is the substrate for complex-level, cancer-context-specific dependency analyses (lineage, driver and genome-state contexts; within-complex genetic interactions; sub-module divergence) whose discoveries are confirmed in a held-out lockbox of cell lines. Here we report the test evaluation, the construction, its null bridge endpoint and the positive controls. We then report the discovery stage of the context analyses, read against registered calibrations: matched random gene sets show most complex-level context associations to be context-wide rather than complex-specific, and a complex-specific subset and a small within-complex genetic-interaction panel remain. Finally, we report the single read of the lockbox: confirmation rates read against what the lockbox's power predicts, a registered benchmark of complex scores against their best single gene, and a second bridge endpoint (BRIDGE-L) that compares lockbox confirmation of discoveries made on the zavg and GLS maps. We also report a defect in the confirmation code that we found after the read, and how it was handled.

### 2. A curation-free complex map

#### 2.1 Construction set, gene universe and graph

The DepMap screens (26Q3; 1,345 non-engineered lines in U) were split, before any dependency value was read, into a lockbox L of 338 lines and a construction set A of 1,007 lines, PatientID-blocked and stratified by lineage and 18 genotype labels. <!-- results/part2/lockbox/manifest.json: n_U, n_L, n_A, genotype_labels, n_patients_straddling --> All construction used A only. The zavg_BEF score was recomputed on A with the locked configuration over S, 6,022 genes with effect < −0.5 in ≥ 1% of A lines and ≥ 95% non-missing. <!-- results/part2/construction/zavg_map/parameters.json: S.n, S.rule --> Edges joined mutual top-k neighbours with score ≥ z0, and complexes were defined by consensus Leiden clustering over 20 PatientID-blocked 80% subsamples of A, multi-resolution persistence and top-down sub-module splitting (Methods). <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3; results/part2/construction/DECISIONS.md D1, D2 -->

#### 2.2 Selection of the graph parameters, reported with its flat criterion

The edge threshold z0 ∈ {4, 5, 6} and neighbourhood size k ∈ {10, 20} were selected by a pre-registered, label-free split-half recovery statistic over four PatientID-blocked halvings of A (Fig. 2c). <!-- results/part2/construction/DECISIONS.md D6 --> The statistic chose z0 = 6, k = 10 (0.343, SD over splits 0.005). <!-- results/part2/construction/selection/selection.json: zavg_BEF.selected, grid[z0=6,k=10].stat_sd --> The criterion was flat across z0 at k = 10: z0 = 4 gave 0.341 (SD 0.010) and z0 = 5 gave 0.338 (SD 0.019), so the choice of z0 is not well determined by the data; k = 10 was clearly preferred over k = 20 (0.204–0.256). <!-- selection.json: zavg_BEF.grid --> A degree-rewired graph scored 0 at every grid point. <!-- selection.json: zavg_BEF.grid[*].rewired_stat --> The GLS comparator map used the same S and k with the edge count matched to the zavg graph of the same model set; GLS's own split-half preference, (4, 10) at 0.315, is reported only. <!-- selection.json: gls.grid; results/part2/construction/README.md "Selection" -->

This calculation was not registered and is not a test. The median shrinkage factor was 0.74 for H3a and 0.59 for H3b. <!-- RV §4; LD: power_shrink_median --> The expected rate was 0.47–0.52 for H3a (observed 0.525) and 0.43–0.44 for H3b (observed 0.40–0.49) (Fig. 5a, grey bands). <!-- LD: power_band; CMP --> **Confirmation is therefore in line with the lockbox's power.** That roughly half the discoveries confirm is not evidence that they fail to replicate beyond what selection predicts. Nor is it evidence that they replicate better than their shrunken A effects predict. <!-- RV §4 "Reading" -->

H3a discoveries that beat all their matched gene sets were confirmed more often than the rest: 287 of 502 (0.572) against 284 of 581 (0.489). <!-- CMP: H3a beats_all_matched_sets, not_beats_all --> Set-beating discoveries have larger A effects, however, and the power aid predicts 0.52–0.53 against 0.41–0.43. <!-- LD: power_band H3a beats_all / not_beats_all --> In H3b the observed split is 0.583 against 0.341 among evaluable fits, and 0.511 against 0.360 in the corrected relook, against an expected 0.50–0.51 and 0.29–0.30. Thirty of these H3b calls are defect-sensitive. <!-- CMP: H3b beats_all_matched_sets, not_beats_all; LD: power_band --> **The gap is about the size that A effect size and L power alone predict, so it is not evidence that complex-specific discoveries replicate better.** The H3b version of the contrast cannot support a claim. <!-- RV MUST-4, MUST-1 -->

## Discussion

This study tested one label-free co-essentiality score through two registered stages: a single look at withheld co-complex pairs, and a single read of held-out cell lines for every complex-level claim built on it. The outcome is mixed, and we state both sides.

**The method wins the test it was built for.** On held-out hu.MAP 3.0 complexes, zavg_BEF beat GLS, Spearman and Pearson under a decision rule frozen before the look. The gain over GLS (+0.121 AP) has an interval that excludes 0 and holds on protein-new pairs. <!-- TR: results.itt.bootstrap.cluster.diff; results.stratum_C3 --> Two qualifications define its scope. First, the gain is carried by complexes with many positive pairs: per-cluster macro AP equals GLS's, and for clusters with five or fewer positive pairs the locked score is not better (§1.5). Second, the method was selected on hu.MAP training labels derived from Complex Portal, so agreement with curated complexes partly re-measures that selection. <!-- DESIGN.md F3 --> As a pair-ranking tool for large complexes, the result supports zavg_BEF over the correlation baselines.

**Its advantage does not carry over to complexes.** We registered two bridges from the pair-level score to the complex map, and neither held. The primary bridge, BioPlex precision of the zavg map against a GLS map at matched coverage, was null (−0.025, −0.075 to +0.031), and protein co-abundance favoured GLS. <!-- BR: primary; secondary.protein_coabundance --> The secondary bridge, BRIDGE-L, was negative: context associations found on the zavg map were confirmed in the lockbox less often than those found on the GLS map (−0.065, −0.107 to −0.025). The difference was the same at matched gene coverage and among physically supported clusters. <!-- BLR7: primary_evaluable --> The post hoc locus-cluster explanation offered for the first bridge does not account for the second. On present evidence, the zavg map's value lies in its larger gene coverage and reproducibility, not in better complexes, and a user who wants context-replicable modules would do at least as well with GLS modules. We do not know why the better pair-level score yields no better map. One possibility, which we have not tested, is that the gain on pair-rich complexes is already captured by any reasonable clustering, while the zavg map's extra coverage adds weaker modules.

**Most complex-level context associations are not about complexes.** On the construction set, random gene sets matched for size, essentiality and coherence were associated with lineages and drivers as often as real complexes (28.1% vs 28.1%; 11.6% vs 12.0%). Even gene sets that are never expressed showed a lineage excess. <!-- DR/*/calibration_D8.json --> A lineage or genotype contrast therefore moves almost any coherent, essentiality-matched gene set. A complex-level dependency atlas, ours included, mostly records gene-set-generic lineage and driver effects, such as shifts in the Chronos scale (the lymphoid scale effect, §4.5), screen-quality differences and broad changes in essential-gene dependency. Earlier module-level lineage analyses did not calibrate against such matched sets [Wainberg 2021; Chen 2023]. We cannot say how much of their signal is complex-specific, and our results suggest that the question needs this calibration.

**About half the associations replicate, which is what the lockbox's power predicts.** In 338 held-out lines, 52.5% of lineage associations and 40–49% of driver associations were confirmed. <!-- CMP: H3a, H3b overall --> The post hoc power aid predicts 47–52% and 43–44% once the selected A effects are shrunk. <!-- LD: power_band --> The discoveries are thus neither failing nor over-performing: they replicate about as often as true effects of their shrunken size would. Complex-specific discoveries were confirmed more often (0.57 vs 0.49 in H3a), but power predicts a gap of about this size, so the lockbox adds no independent evidence that beating matched sets marks real complex-level biology. <!-- CMP: H3a beats_all; LD: power_band; RV MUST-4 --> The registered benchmark makes the same point from another side: complex scores did not replicate better than their best single gene, and where few members carry the association, the single gene replicated clearly better (§7.6). For most context dependencies, then, the complex is not the right unit.

**What survives.** The strongest specific findings pass every check we were able to apply: they were confirmed in the lockbox, beat all their matched gene sets in A, lie outside locus clusters and are not defect-sensitive. The registered per-hit leave-one-lineage-out check has not been run (§7.2). There are 261 lineage and 29 driver associations of this kind (231 and 23 also robust to both A-side sensitivities). <!-- LD: H3a.n_strongest, H3b.n_strongest, n_strongest_screen_and_gpd_kept --> They include higher lymphoid dependency on a unit including ADSL, ATIC, CAD, CTPS1, DHODH, GART, GMPS and IMPDH2, and on the eight haem-synthesis enzymes ALAD–UROS. They also include higher bowel dependency on a unit including CTNNB1, TCF7L2, HNF1A, HNF4A, GATA6 and FOXA2, and higher Head and Neck dependency on an integrin unit including ITGB1, ITGA3 and CD151. Among driver contexts, RB1-LoF lines depend more on a unit including CCNE1, CCNE2, CDK2, E2F1, E2F3 and SKP2, and TP53-altered lines on a unit including ATM, TP53BP1, SHLD1 and SHLD2 (§7.2). Read post hoc from the gene symbols, several of these match known biology: nucleotide and haem synthesis in lymphoid lines, intestinal lineage transcription, and the cyclin E–CDK2 axis after RB1 loss as the counterpart of the registered CDK4/6 control. They are best read as replicated, gene-set-controlled associations at the level of a module. Neither the matched-set comparison nor the lockbox shows that the complex, rather than one or two of its members, is the operative unit, and none has been tested outside DepMap.

**Within complexes, the common outcome is no change.** The H1 panel recovered known paralog compensation. ARID1A loss → ARID1B was confirmed in the lockbox, but it is registered control PC6 [Helming 2014]. EP300 loss → CREBBP, the reciprocal of a published dependency [Ogiwara 2016], passed only the rare-context route on 4 lockbox loss lines. <!-- LB/h1_events_primary_vs_corrected_R7.tsv --> The FOCAD signal is the PELO–HBS1L dependency already registered as PC10 [Prindle 2025]; it is defect-sensitive in the lockbox and carries no claim. TP53 and PTEN gave detectable effects inside the equivalence margin, and confirmation cannot promote them. The informative H1 result is the neutral one: 11 of 24 tumour-suppressor and other genetic-loss events left the dependency of the rest of their complex unchanged within ±0.1 Chronos units, and their lockbox estimates are consistent with that. <!-- PD: H1.n_neutral; LD: H1_lockbox --> Loss of one subunit, even a tumour suppressor, does not as a rule rewire the dependency of its complex. Where something does change, it is a single paralog or a single partner, as the member-resolved profiles show (Fig. 4c), not the complex as a unit. This agrees with the view that paralog buffering acts within complexes [De Kegel & Ryan 2019], and it bounds how much complex-level rewiring one should expect from DepMap.

**Process.** Three procedural points matter for how far these results can be trusted.
- The single look and single read held, and every confirmatory number was reproduced by someone who did not produce it.
- The calibrations that changed our reading the most, matched random sets and never-expressed sets, were registered but implemented only after the discovery runs (D8). This was disclosed, and their responses were applied mechanically.
- A defect in the lockbox driver was found only after the read, although the dress rehearsal had shown the same failures. Keeping the read as executed as the primary result, labelling every call that the correction changes as defect-sensitive, and reporting the corrected relook alongside was the conservative choice. It costs the WGD context and four controls their lockbox evidence.

Our main procedural lesson is that a rehearsal is useful only if its outputs are checked, fit status included.

**Limitations** are listed below. The most important are these:
- All dependencies come from one CRISPR resource, and the rare-context route is not an independent replication.
- The lockbox is small, so non-confirmation of small effects is often uninformative, and L cannot re-establish equivalence for the neutral H1 events.
- "Complex" means a co-dependency module supported by physical or proteomic evidence, not a curated complex.
- Several rules (E2, the defect-sensitive label, the power aid) were fixed after outcomes were seen. All of them make the reading more conservative, and all are listed in the deviations table.

## Conclusions

1. A label-free co-complex score, locked before the test data existed, outperformed Pearson, Spearman and GLS on held-out hu.MAP 3.0 complexes in a single pre-registered look. The gain is concentrated in complexes with many positive pairs.
2. The advantage did not carry over to complexes. The zavg map did not agree better with BioPlex than a GLS map (null), and its context discoveries were confirmed in held-out lines less often than the GLS map's (negative). Both bridges were pre-registered.
3. Complex-level context associations in DepMap largely reflect gene-set-generic lineage and driver effects: matched random gene sets show them as often as real complexes. About half confirm in held-out lines, which is the rate the lockbox's power predicts. Complex scores did not replicate better than their best single gene.
4. Within complexes, known paralog compensation was recovered (ARID1A → ARID1B, a registered control; EP300 → CREBBP, rare-context route only). Most tumour-suppressor losses left their complex's dependency unchanged.
5. The strongest specific findings are 261 lineage and 29 driver associations that were confirmed in the lockbox, beat matched gene sets, lie outside locus clusters and do not depend on the code defect. Examples are lymphoid nucleotide- and haem-synthesis units, a bowel CTNNB1/TCF7L2/HNF4A unit, and a CCNE1/CCNE2/CDK2 unit in RB1-LoF lines, with functional names given post hoc. <!-- LD: H3a.n_strongest, H3b.n_strongest; manuscript/tables/tableS7_lockbox_h3.md --> They are candidates for testing outside DepMap, and they remain provisional until the registered per-hit robustness check has been run. Any complex-level dependency analysis should test complex-specificity against matched gene sets rather than assume it.

### The locked method

The co-complex score zavg_BEF and its development are described in the Part 1 repository, locked at commit c7f95ee007576be9311c0a5f08c6ac63ca23a009 (28 October 2026, 12:55:43 UTC) and used from a read-only clone. <!-- DATA.md "Part 1 (locked method)"; docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §1 --> In brief, zavg_BEF is the equal-weight mean of the three component z-scores (Part 1 commit c7f95ee, `src/predict.py`; the run records' "model.predict" is Part 1's generic predictor interface), with components computed on gene-standardised CRISPRGeneEffect profiles with the top 20 principal components removed and ridge whitening: first-order correlation (F), second-order neighbourhood cosine (B) and a Laplacian-eigenmap cosine (E). <!-- LITERATURE.md §0; results/test_eval/score_records/test/zavg_BEF.run.json: method.component_names --> The score has no parameters fitted to labels; its configuration was selected on hu.MAP training pairs by complex-grouped cross-validation, with one internal final test (IFT) built from training pairs. <!-- docs/part1_audit.md verdict 5 --> Development reference values were dev HT-AP 0.470 (GLS 0.312) and IFT HT-AP 0.345 (GLS 0.242). <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §4 "Reference values" --> Proteins were mapped UniProt → HGNC → Entrez → DepMap column as in Part 1. <!-- DATA.md "Notes" --> The baselines are Part 1's development-selected variants: signed Pearson and Spearman correlation (pairwise complete) and signed GLS t-statistics [Wainberg 2021] without olfactory-receptor PC removal, with Part 1's exact marginal-covariance missing-data protocol. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §2 --> The secondary candidate B is the second-order component alone, and the post hoc reference is Pearson correlation after removal of 20 PCs without whitening. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §2; score record component_names --> Nothing learned in this study changed the method (firewall F1). <!-- DESIGN.md F1 -->

### Test protocol

The protocol (docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md v1.2) was revised through three design-review rounds and frozen before the look. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md header, §9 -->

*Pair set.* Pairs were canonicalised as unordered UniProt pairs; self pairs and pairs in both files were excluded (none existed); pairs also in a training file were kept (none existed). <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §3.1; TR: audit --> Unmapped pairs (33,931 of 600,738) were kept at the predictor's floor score, min(mapped scores) − 1, as one tied bottom block for every method (intention-to-score, ITT). <!-- TR: results.itt.n_unmapped_any; docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md R5 --> S1 is the set of pairs mapped under every method, Part 1's evaluation universe. Strata C1/C2/C3 contain pairs with both, one or neither protein among training-positive proteins. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §3.2 --> One ID-only file per set was scored, so no label reached the predictor. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §1 "Labels" -->

*Metrics.* The primary metric is non-interpolated average precision at natural prevalence, computed with Part 1's metric functions (all pair weights 1, so Part 1's Horvitz–Thompson-weighted AP equals plain AP) [Saito & Rehmsmeier 2015; Davis & Goadrich 2006; Boyd 2013]. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §4 --> Secondary metrics are AUROC; precision at recall 0.10, 0.25 and 0.50; AP with hard negatives only; macro per-cluster AP; AP without the largest component and without the 10 largest clusters; AP in essentiality strata; and precision, recall and F1 at three pre-specified rules. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §5 --> Rule (b), the primary operating point, is the threshold maximising F1 over all hu.MAP training pairs; rule (c) predicts the top K = round(π_train × N) pairs; rule (a) is z ≥ 3 for zavg_BEF only and is non-blind (D1). <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §5.4 --> Test clusters are connected components of the test-positive graph, with components > 40 proteins split by Louvain (none needed splitting), and singleton clusters for proteins without positives. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §5.2; TR: results.itt.cluster_stats.components_split --> [Blondel 2008]

*Calibration.* Each method's scores were mapped to probabilities by isotonic regression (primary) and Platt scaling (secondary) fitted on all 634,779 hu.MAP training pairs and applied unchanged [Zadrozny & Elkan 2002; Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana 2005]. Brier score, Brier skill (against the train prevalence), calibration-in-the-large, calibration intercept and slope, ECE and reliability diagrams with log-spaced bins were reported [Van Calster 2019]. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §5.5 -->

*Uncertainty.* The primary interval is Part 1's paired cluster bootstrap: per replicate, cluster multiplicities m = bincount(integers(0, C, C)) with `numpy.random.default_rng(0)`, a within-cluster pair weighted m_c and a between-cluster pair m_c1·m_c2; B = 2,000; 95% percentile intervals; two-sided p = 2·min(P(Δ ≤ 0), P(Δ ≥ 0)) [Field & Welsh 2007]. Protein (node) and stratified pair bootstraps are secondary, the latter anti-conservative; Monte-Carlo seeds 1–4 show seed dependence [Owen 2007]. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §6 --> Thresholds and calibrators were not refitted within replicates, so their sampling error is not propagated.

*Decision rule.* zavg_BEF "clearly beats" the baselines if, against each of GLS, Spearman and Pearson, the relative AP gain is ≥ 10% (point estimate) and macro per-cluster AP and AP without the largest component are ≥ 0.95× the baseline's (intersection–union), computed by Part 1's unchanged `final_eval.decide` on S1. B was confirmatory only if zavg_BEF passed (fixed sequence). The CI-excludes-0 statistic, Holm-adjusted p-values and non-degradation ratio CIs were pre-specified and reported but do not alter the decision. zavg_BEF was to remain the Part 2 method whatever the outcome, and outcome readings were pre-written. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §7 -->

*Single-look procedure.* The freeze marker hashed the protocol, fitted rules, evaluation code and tests, package list and test files; every test-touching entry point verified it. The look required a committed intent, committed a "started" record before reading labels, and refused if any earlier look existed on disk, in any ref or in the reflog; any re-run would be automatically non-confirmatory. Commit hashes and the SHA-256 of the results were reported to the user outside the agents' reach. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §8.2–8.4; RESEARCH_TREE.md P1 --> These measures are procedural: a process with file access could read the labels, so the guarantee rests on the agent briefs, the disclosure of all prior contact and the externally reported hashes. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §8.4 "Residual limits" -->

*Verification.* Verifier A re-implemented the protocol without importing the evaluation package; Verifier B re-scored a 1% sample (seed 7 instead of the protocol's seed 0, as briefed; disclosed deviation). <!-- results/test_eval/verify/README.md, "Deviation from §8.3" --> Before the freeze, a dry run reproduced Part 1's IFT look to ≤ 1.5 × 10⁻⁸ and 34 unit tests passed. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §10 -->

### Complex construction

*Scores.* zavg_BEF was recomputed on A with the locked configuration, null moments recomputed per run by the locked procedure; on all U lines the recomputation reproduces Part 1 scores to ≤ 1e-12. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3 "Scores"; RESEARCH_TREE.md "Construction infrastructure" --> Score blocks were computed on A, on 20 PatientID-blocked 80% subsamples (seeds 1000–1019) and on 4 lineage-stratified split halves (seeds 2000–2003). <!-- results/part2/construction/README.md "Pipeline" -->

*Gene set S.* Genes with effect < −0.5 in ≥ 1% of A lines, non-missing in ≥ 95%, and not low-coverage in Part 1 (6,022 genes). <!-- zavg_map/parameters.json: S -->

*Graph.* An edge joins genes whose score is ≥ z0 and that are mutual top-k neighbours within S; weight = min(score, z0 + 3) − z0 + 1. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3 "Graph"; zavg_map/parameters.json: weight_cap -->

*Consensus and persistence.* Co-clustering frequencies over 20 subsamples × 5 Leiden seeds were clustered with Leiden-CPM over the γ grid 0.05·√2^i up to 1.6 [Traag 2019; Traag 2011; Fortunato & Barthélemy 2007; Monti 2003; Lancichinetti & Fortunato 2012; Jeub 2018; Zheng 2021 (HiDeF)]. <!-- zavg_map/parameters.json: cluster_config.gammas, seeds --> A cluster is persistent when it recurs (Jaccard ≥ 0.75) over ≥ 3 adjacent γ values with support ≥ 0.6, where support in a subsample run is containment of ≥ 75% of its members in one run cluster (decision D1, chosen on a toy simulation before any map was built because Jaccard support lost 3–4-gene complexes). <!-- DECISIONS.md D1; parameters.json: jaccard_recur, support_min, support_mode, min_adjacent --> Candidates were placed most-persistent-first, with nested candidates kept as sub-modules, duplicates dropped and partial overlaps trimmed or resolved to the more persistent cluster (D2). <!-- DECISIONS.md D2 --> Level-1 complexes have 3–60 members and all-pairs mean score ≥ z0; sub-modules come from top-down re-clustering inside each complex at γ = 0.5 and need mean score ≥ z0 + 1. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3; parameters.json: gamma2, min_size, max_size, cohesion_delta --> On the toy simulation, the chosen rules recovered 86 of 105 planted complexes (25 of 30 of size 3–4) and all 39 planted sub-modules, and returned no cluster on null data. <!-- DECISIONS.md "Toy results" (V2_containment_nested) -->

*Selection.* For each (z0, k) ∈ {4, 5, 6} × {10, 20}, each half of four PatientID-blocked lineage-stratified splits was clustered through the same pipeline; non-locus half-1 clusters of size 5–30 count as recovered when a half-2 cluster matches with Jaccard ≥ 0.5, and the statistic is the selective-gene coverage of recovered clusters, averaged over both directions and the four splits. The highest mean was selected (ties to higher z0, then lower k), and a degree-rewired baseline was reported. <!-- DECISIONS.md D6; selection.json: rule -->

*GLS comparator.* For each run, the GLS graph uses the same S and k and the same edge count as the zavg graph of that model set, with weights quantile-matched and cohesion applied on the rank-mapped scale, so both maps remove the same score quantile. <!-- DECISIONS.md D4 -->

*Locus flag and attachment.* A cluster is a locus cluster if ≥ 50% of its members lie within 10 Mb (Ensembl coordinates) or on one chromosome arm. Genes outside S are attached to a cluster when ≥ ⌈|C|/2⌉ members score ≥ z0 with the gene and a hypergeometric null gives BH q < 0.01 over all gene × cluster tests; attachments to locus clusters, on the cluster's dominant arm or within 10 Mb of a member are flagged and cannot define H1 events. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3; DECISIONS.md D3 -->

### Tiers

T1: within-cluster BioPlex edges (293T ∪ HCT116, Entrez-mapped; ≥ 3 observed members, ≥ 2 edges) above a null replacing each observed member by a random S gene of the same log2 BioPlex degree bin and A-essentiality class (10⁴ draws), BH q < 0.05 within level. T2 (not T1): mean pairwise protein correlation among ≥ 3 measured members (A lines only, ≥ 20 lines per protein) above a size-matched null (10⁴ draws), BH q < 0.05. T3-neg: testable by either source but not T1/T2; T3-nc otherwise. <!-- DECISIONS.md D5 "Tiers"; tiers/summary.json: rules --> Clusters in T1 or T2 are called complexes and the rest co-dependency modules. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3 "Tiers" -->

### Bridge endpoint

The unit is the level-1 cluster, including locus clusters. Within each size bin {3–4, 5–8, 9–16, 17–60}, precision is pooled over within-cluster pairs with both proteins BioPlex-observed; bins are averaged with weights given by the pooled zavg + GLS cluster-size distribution after coverage matching (0.306, 0.269, 0.306, 0.119). <!-- DECISIONS.md D5; BR: primary.bin_weights --> The map with more genes drops its least stable clusters until coverage matches. The CI is a protein-node bootstrap (B = 2,000, seed 5000) with clusters fixed and each pair weighted by the product of its nodes' multiplicities; "better" requires the 95% CI to exclude 0. Sensitivities (no matching; locus clusters excluded) and secondary endpoints (co-abundance, seed 5001; stability by size; pair-level AP against BioPlex; BRIDGE-L) were fixed before validation. <!-- DECISIONS.md D5 --> Map-to-map variability was added after the map freeze as the descriptive analysis DESIGN requires, without changing the frozen maps; random size-matched references used seed 7000. <!-- results/part2/construction/README.md "Map-to-map variability"; results/part2/verify/fzc_review.md §5 -->

### Complex scores and association statistics

*Complex score.* S_C(m) is the mean over the fixed members of C of CRISPRGeneEffect, each value clipped to [−3, 3]; for a given test, the context gene(s), their paralogues within C and members within ±10 Mb of a copy-number context gene are removed; S_C is missing if fewer than half the remaining members are observed. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: score_rule; DESIGN.md §2.4 --> Alternative scores (eigengene, within-line rank, expression-masked, pan-essential-referenced) and leave-one-lineage-out and leave-top-2-out robustness are reported per hit and never used for selection. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.4 -->

*Model.* S_C ~ context + covariates by ordinary least squares: library multi-hot, ScreenType3DO and ModelType for all tests; lineage fixed effects for non-lineage contexts; log mutation burden and MSIScore for mutation contexts; ploidy, WGD and TP53 status for copy-number contexts. No covariate that is a function of the context is used. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.5, §2.2 "Covariate rule" --> *Lineage-merge convention:* indicator levels (for example OncotreeLineage levels) with fewer than two lines in the analysed sample are merged into the reference level. This engine convention is not stated in DESIGN §2.5 and was noted by the independent controls review; with full lineage fixed effects instead, β changes by ≤ 10⁻³ (relative) and p by ≤ 2% (relative) in the controls. <!-- results/part2/verify/controls_review.md §2 "One convention difference", note 2 -->

*Test statistic.* The context coefficient is tested with HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors [MacKinnon & White 1985, M4; Long & Ervin 2000, M5] and Satterthwaite (Bell–McCaffrey) degrees of freedom [Satterthwaite 1946, M6; Bell & McCaffrey 2002, M7]. A (context, unit) test is evaluable with ≥ 12 context and ≥ 12 reference A lines after complete-case filtering and ≥ 2 members after removals. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: statistic --> The HC3 implementation matches statsmodels. <!-- RESEARCH_TREE.md "FZ-LB lockbox ... stats machinery" -->

*Permutation.* Screening hits (BB-adjusted q < 0.1) are re-tested by exact PatientID-blocked Freedman–Lane permutation [Winkler 2014] with 10,000 permutations, within lineage for non-lineage contexts and unrestricted for lineage contexts; the p-value is (k + 1)/(B + 1), and a generalised Pareto tail approximation [Knijnenburg 2009, M8] is used only when that value falls below 10⁻⁴. Permutation p-values re-enter and the FDR procedure is re-run. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: statistic; results/part2/verify/h3a_fdr_diagnosis.md "Fix" --> An earlier implementation applied the tail approximation whenever there were fewer than 10 exceedances with 1,000 permutations, which inflated the size of the test by 30–80% at the relevant thresholds in simulation; it was corrected to the registered rule on synthetic data, before any real association was run. <!-- results/part2/verify/h3a_fdr_diagnosis.md "Evidence" §1, "Fix" --> In simulation the Freedman–Lane test held its type-I error at 0.05. <!-- RESEARCH_TREE.md "FZ-LB ... FL type-I 0.05" -->

*Error control.* Error control is Benjamini–Bogomolov selective FDR within each direction [Benjamini & Bogomolov 2014, M1], not pooled FDR (decision D6): families are contexts; a family's p-value is the Simes combination over its units [Simes 1986, M2]; families are selected by BH at q over the M families [Benjamini & Hochberg 1995]; within each of the R selected families BH is applied at q·R/M. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: fdr_rule; RESEARCH_TREE.md D6 --> This controls the expected average, over selected families, of the within-family false discovery proportion. In 1,000 simulations with planted lineage effects, the BB error rate was 0.046 ± 0.002 at q = 0.05, while the pooled FDP across all discoveries was 0.068 ± 0.002; the pooled figure is reported but not claimed. Under the global null, the probability of any discovery was 0.020 (H3a) and 0.023 (H3b). <!-- results/part2/verify/h3a_fdr_diagnosis.md "Validation" --> Sub-modules are tested stage-wise only within complexes that pass [Van den Berge 2017]. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.5 "FDR" --> Holm's procedure [Holm 1979, M3] was used only for the three primary ΔAP comparisons of the test evaluation.

*Calibration and negative controls.* Per direction, 100 pseudo-contexts matched to real contexts in size and lineage composition (seed 4600), random gene sets matched for size, essentiality and coherence, and dispersed never-expressed sets are run through the same machinery; if the fraction of pseudo-context tests with p < 0.05 exceeds 0.075, every real test with p < 0.01 is re-tested by permutation. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: calibration --> Only the pseudo-contexts were implemented when the discovery runs were made. ## Limitations

*Part 1 (test evaluation)*
1. **The single-look guarantee is procedural.** The test labels were plain files, and label-free genome-wide scores covering the test pairs already existed. Incident D1 shows that the risk was real, and one operating point is non-blind as a result. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §0, §8.4 -->
2. **The gain is carried by pair-rich clusters.** Macro per-cluster AP does not differ from GLS's. In the descriptive size breakdown, made after the look, zavg_BEF is not better than GLS for clusters with ≤ 5 positive pairs. The test has 238 clusters with a Kish effective number of 21.3, so CIs are wide for Spearman and Pearson. <!-- TR: results.itt.cluster_stats -->
3. **Training-label re-measurement.** zavg_BEF was selected on hu.MAP training labels derived from Complex Portal, so agreement with physical complexes partly re-measures that selection. <!-- DESIGN.md F3 -->
4. **Calibration.** Calibration-in-the-large of the train-fitted isotonic calibrator was 0.893 on the test set, with no CI pre-specified. No recalibration was made, and the calibrators' own sampling error is not propagated. <!-- TR: results.itt.metrics.zavg_BEF.calibration.isotonic.calibration_in_the_large -->

*Complex map and bridges*
5. **Both bridges failed.** The BioPlex bridge is null, protein co-abundance favours GLS, and BRIDGE-L is negative (−0.065, −0.107 to −0.025). No statement that the zavg map is the better map has support. The locus-cluster explanation of the BioPlex deficit is post hoc, and it does not explain BRIDGE-L. <!-- BR; BLR7: primary_evaluable -->
6. **Selection is flat across z0** at k = 10, so a different z0 could have been selected on the same evidence.
7. **Locus clusters are frequent** (28% at level 1). They are flagged, not removed, and T2 is not locus-aware.
8. **Stability has no chance baseline.** The variability analysis shares subsamples with the frozen map, and its random reference is a floor.
9. **Some complexes merge two complexes or are pathway modules** (for example PRC1 + PRC2), so "complex" means physically supported module.
10. **Coverage of the map.** 2,380 of the 6,022 S genes (about 40%) are in no level-1 complex, including SMARCA2, STAG1 and KIF18A, which limits paralog-based analyses. <!-- summary.json: zavg.genes_S − zavg.genes_level1; results/part2/fzx/README.md "Controls" -->

*Context associations (H3a, H3b, H2)*
11. **Most complex-level context associations are not complex-specific.** Matched random gene sets reach p < 0.05 as often as real complexes, so the 1,306 H3a/H3b discoveries mostly describe contexts, not complexes. The "beats all matched sets" flag rests on only 10 sets per complex, which makes it a coarse screen. 69 discoveries have no matched sets, and the 95% filter is post hoc. Selective FDR controls the average within-family error over selected families, not the pooled error, which was 0.068 in simulation. <!-- DR/*/calibration_D8.json; DR/SPECIFICITY.md; results/part2/verify/h3a_fdr_diagnosis.md -->
12. **Calibrations implemented after the discovery runs.** The matched sets, never-expressed sets, segment null and H2 random splits were registered but not implemented when the discovery runs were made, and they ran after those outcomes were known (D8). The literal reading of the response scope was fixed before the calibrations ran and confirmed after their results were seen. A broader reading would have fired a response that adds re-tests but removes none.
13. **GPD tail.** Permutation p-values below 10⁻⁴ are extrapolated and can lie many orders of magnitude below the analytic p. Using the larger of the two drops 32 H3a, 31 H3b (16 of 26 in WGD), 2 H1S and 2 H2 discoveries. Three single-discovery H3b families are selected only through the tail. <!-- DR/gpd_sensitivity_summary.json; results/part2/verify/discovery_review.md review 1 M1 -->
14. **Screen quality and scale.** About a quarter of H3a/H3b discoveries are lost when screen-quality covariates are added, though that analysis also drops 119 lines. Esophagus/Stomach is fragile (2 of 30 kept, 6 of 30 confirmed in L), as are WGD (12 of 26) and MSI-H (7 of 14). The lymphoid association between complex essentiality and β (ρ −0.24) persists after adjustment and suggests a Chronos dynamic-range effect: 21 of the 33 lymphoid "strongest specific" entries have β > 0. Never-expressed sets show a residual lineage artefact (13.5% at p < 0.05). <!-- DR/SENSITIVITY_SCREEN.md; LD: H3a.confirmed_per_context, n_strongest_lymphoid_positive_beta -->
15. **Per-hit robustness and multiverse not computed.** The registry's strongest-finding rule requires each hit to be robust to leaving out each lineage (or primary disease), and lists alternative scores (eigengene, within-line rank, expression-masked, pan-essential-referenced) and leave-top-2-out as per-hit reports. These were deferred at the discovery runs and have not been run, so the "strongest specific" set is provisional on that criterion. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: success_criterion, multiverse; DR/H3a_lineage_atlas/INTENT.md item 5 -->
16. **H2 is exploratory and mostly reproduced by random splits** (78.3 vs 109 stage-wise rejections). Its parents were chosen from H3 discoveries that are themselves mostly not complex-specific. Lockbox confirmation (0.70) shows that the divergence recurs, not that it is specific to the sub-module, and the rank-1 subset was not confirmed more often.

*H1*
17. **The H1 headline events re-find known biology.** ARID1A → ARID1B is also positive control PC6, and FOCAD → PELO–HBS1L overlaps control PC10. EP300 → CREBBP rests on 12 A loss lines at the evaluability floor, a module without physical support (T3-neg) and the rare-context route (4 L loss lines). The FOCAD lockbox call is defect-sensitive.
18. **Neutral H1 events rest on A.** L has a median of 8 loss lines per neutral event, and only 2 of 11 L 90% CIs lie inside ±0.1, so L does not re-establish equivalence. <!-- LD: H1_lockbox -->
19. **Thin control coverage.** H3a rests on two controls, the H1 pass on one multi-gene readout, and H2 has none. Five controls were not evaluable at complex level. In L, four controls (PC9, PC10, PC12, PC13) are defect-sensitive and cannot be claimed as recovered.
20. **Amendments and post hoc rules.** A1 and A2 changed the H1 panel before any H1 run, from labels only. E1 corrected a sign typo after the run, matching the code that ran. E2 (the within-margin label for TP53 and PTEN) and the 95% specificity filter are post hoc. The PTEN call depends on the evaluability-adjusted m (q = 0.044 at m = 24, 0.051 at the registered 28). The member-resolved profiles were exported after the discovery run, from A only, and were committed during the lockbox read window.

*Lockbox*
21. **A defect in the confirmation driver.** The L covariate design was built over all models and then subset, so 53 fits failed and 510 shifted slightly. It was found only after the read, although the dress rehearsal had shown the same failures. The handling rules (R6, R7) were made after the outcomes were seen. They keep the read as executed as primary and strip claims from all 53 calls that the correction changes, but they cost H3b's WGD context and four controls their lockbox evidence. The H3b rates depend on how the failures are counted (0.40–0.49).
22. **Lockbox size and power.** L has 338 lines, about a third of A. Several contexts and H1 events have fewer than 5 L context lines and take the rare-context route, which re-uses A lines and DEMETER2 and is not an independent replication. Non-confirmation of small effects is often uninformative (325 of 1,145 H3a discoveries have |β| < 0.05), and the median lineage discovery had 19 L context lines. <!-- PD: H3a.n_abs_beta_lt_0.05; RVD/tables_review.json: power.H3a_lineage_atlas.median_nc_L -->
23. **The power aid is post hoc.** It was computed after the read by the reviewer, is not registered, pools an empirical-Bayes prior across contexts, treats correlated tests as independent and uses normal approximations. It frames the rates and is not a test. <!-- RV §4 "Caveats" -->
24. **BENCH.** 16.5% of bootstrap pair × replicate fits fail and are dropped. The high-tercile CI depends on how the covariate design is assembled within a replicate (lower bound −0.033 or −0.122). Within-line-rank scoring and the A–L random-set correlation were not run (A3–A5), and the multiverse is point estimates only. Breadth is taken over all members, including those removed from the score (a literal reading, A5). <!-- RVD/bench_recompute.json; AMENDMENTS.md A3–A5 -->
25. **The loader count is inferred.** That the lockbox loader was called exactly 30 times rests on ledger invariants and file times, because the run log was not committed. <!-- RV N3 -->

*General*
26. **Single cell-line resource.** All dependencies come from one CRISPR resource (DepMap 26Q3, Chronos), and DEMETER2 enters only the rare-context route. No result has been tested in another screen collection, under different culture conditions or in vivo. DepMap lineages are unevenly represented.
27. **Agent-run study.** Analyses, reviews and rulings were carried out by AI agents under a human principal investigator. Independent agent reviews reproduced every confirmatory number, but the reviewers share a model family with the producers.
