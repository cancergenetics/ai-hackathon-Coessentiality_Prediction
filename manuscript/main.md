## Abstract

**Background.** Genes whose loss-of-function fitness effects co-vary across cancer cell lines (co-essentiality) often encode subunits of the same protein complex, but co-essentiality methods are usually tuned and evaluated on overlapping curated labels, and complex-level analyses of cancer dependencies commonly build on curated complex definitions.

## Introduction

Genome-scale CRISPR loss-of-function screens across large cancer cell-line panels, such as the Cancer Dependency Map (DepMap), measure the fitness effect of knocking out each gene in each line [Tsherniak 2017; Meyers 2017; Dempster 2021 (Chronos); Arafeh 2025]. Subunits of the same protein complex tend to have correlated fitness profiles across lines, so co-essentiality has been used to recover complexes, pathways and the functions of uncharacterised genes [Pan 2018; Boyle 2018; Kim 2019; Wainberg 2021]. Generalised least squares (GLS) on whitened profiles, which removes the dominant line-to-line covariance, is a widely used standard for building co-essential modules [Wainberg 2021].

Two problems limit how far such results can be trusted. First, method development and evaluation frequently share the curated complex labels used to judge success, and pair-input predictions are vulnerable to optimistic evaluation when test pairs share proteins with training pairs [Park & Marcotte 2012]. Second, downstream complex-level analyses of cancer dependencies usually start from curated complex resources (CORUM, Complex Portal) or functional networks (STRING) [Tsitsiridis 2023; Meldal 2022; Szklarczyk 2023], which makes it hard to separate what the dependency data show from what the curation already encoded.

We address both problems with a pre-registered, two-part design. In Part 1, a label-free co-complex score developed and locked in earlier work (zavg_BEF; Part 1 repository commit c7f95ee) <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §0 "Other facts at the freeze" --> was evaluated once on the hu.MAP 3.0 test pairs [Fischer 2025], which were withheld throughout its development, against the conventional baselines used in that development (Pearson, Spearman, GLS). The protocol, decision rule, operating points and calibrators were frozen and hashed before the look, and all prior contact with the test files was disclosed. In Part 2, the locked score, recomputed on a construction subset of DepMap lines, was converted into a hierarchical complex map without any curated complex resource, functional network or hu.MAP prediction. The map was tiered by orthogonal physical (BioPlex 3.0 [Huttlin 2021]) and proteomic (CCLE proteomics [Nusinow 2020]) evidence read only after the map was frozen, and compared with a GLS map of equal coverage in a pre-registered "bridge" endpoint. The map is the substrate for complex-level, cancer-context-specific dependency analyses (lineage, driver and genome-state contexts; within-complex genetic interactions; sub-module divergence) whose discoveries are confirmed in a held-out lockbox of cell lines. Here we report the test evaluation, the construction, its null bridge endpoint and the positive controls; [[RESULTS: one-sentence summary of Part 2 association findings]].

### 2. A curation-free complex map

#### 2.1 Construction set, gene universe and graph

The DepMap screens (26Q3; 1,345 non-engineered lines in U) were split, before any dependency value was read, into a lockbox L of 338 lines and a construction set A of 1,007 lines, PatientID-blocked and stratified by lineage and 18 genotype labels. <!-- results/part2/lockbox/manifest.json: n_U, n_L, n_A, genotype_labels, n_patients_straddling --> All construction used A only. #### 2.2 Selection of the graph parameters, reported with its flat criterion

The edge threshold z0 ∈ {4, 5, 6} and neighbourhood size k ∈ {10, 20} were selected by a pre-registered, label-free split-half recovery statistic over four PatientID-blocked halvings of A (Fig. 2c). <!-- results/part2/construction/DECISIONS.md D6 --> The statistic chose z0 = 6, k = 10 (0.343, SD over splits 0.005). <!-- results/part2/construction/selection/selection.json: zavg_BEF.selected, grid[z0=6,k=10].stat_sd --> The criterion was flat across z0 at k = 10: z0 = 4 gave 0.341 (SD 0.010) and z0 = 5 gave 0.338 (SD 0.019), so the choice of z0 is not well determined by the data; k = 10 was clearly preferred over k = 20 (0.204–0.256). <!-- selection.json: zavg_BEF.grid --> A degree-rewired graph scored 0 at every grid point. <!-- selection.json: zavg_BEF.grid[*].rewired_stat --> The GLS comparator map used the same S and k with the edge count matched to the zavg graph of the same model set; GLS's own split-half preference, (4, 10) at 0.315, is reported only. <!-- selection.json: gls.grid; results/part2/construction/README.md "Selection" -->

#### 2.3 The maps

#### 2.6 The pre-registered bridge endpoint is null

## Discussion

[[DISCUSSION: to be written after the Part 2 association results]]. Points fixed by the results above:
- The locked score's advantage on held-out complexes is robust (CI excludes 0 against all baselines, gain retained on protein-new pairs) but is carried by pair-rich complexes; for the typical small complex it is not better than GLS.

### The locked method

The co-complex score zavg_BEF and its development are described in the Part 1 repository, locked at commit c7f95ee007576be9311c0a5f08c6ac63ca23a009 (28 October 2026, 12:55:43 UTC) and used from a read-only clone. <!-- DATA.md "Part 1 (locked method)"; docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §1 --> In brief, zavg_BEF is the equal-weight mean of three z-scored similarity components computed on gene-standardised CRISPRGeneEffect profiles with the top 20 principal components removed and ridge whitening: first-order correlation (F), second-order neighbourhood cosine (B) and a Laplacian-eigenmap cosine (E). <!-- LITERATURE.md §0; results/test_eval/score_records/test/zavg_BEF.run.json: method.component_names --> The score has no parameters fitted to labels; its configuration was selected on hu.MAP training pairs by complex-grouped cross-validation, with one internal final test (IFT) built from training pairs. <!-- docs/part1_audit.md verdict 5 --> Development reference values were dev HT-AP 0.470 (GLS 0.312) and IFT HT-AP 0.345 (GLS 0.242). <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §4 "Reference values" --> Proteins were mapped UniProt → HGNC → Entrez → DepMap column as in Part 1. <!-- DATA.md "Notes" --> The baselines are Part 1's development-selected variants: signed Pearson and Spearman correlation (pairwise complete) and signed GLS t-statistics [Wainberg 2021] without olfactory-receptor PC removal, with Part 1's exact marginal-covariance missing-data protocol. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §2 --> The secondary candidate B is the second-order component alone, and the post hoc reference is Pearson correlation after removal of 20 PCs without whitening. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §2; score record component_names --> Nothing learned in this study changed the method (firewall F1). <!-- DESIGN.md F1 -->

### Test protocol

The protocol (docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md v1.2) was revised through three design-review rounds and frozen before the look. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md header, §9 -->

*Pair set.* Pairs were canonicalised as unordered UniProt pairs; self pairs and pairs in both files were excluded (none existed); pairs also in a training file were kept (none existed). <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §3.1; TR: audit --> Unmapped pairs (33,931 of 600,738) were kept at the predictor's floor score, min(mapped scores) − 1, as one tied bottom block for every method (intention-to-score, ITT). <!-- TR: results.itt.n_unmapped_any; docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md R5 --> S1 is the set of pairs mapped under every method, Part 1's evaluation universe. Strata C1/C2/C3 contain pairs with both, one or neither protein among training-positive proteins. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §3.2 --> One ID-only file per set was scored, so no label reached the predictor. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §1 "Labels" -->

*Metrics.* The primary metric is non-interpolated average precision at natural prevalence, computed with Part 1's metric functions (all pair weights 1, so Part 1's Horvitz–Thompson-weighted AP equals plain AP) [Saito & Rehmsmeier 2015; Davis & Goadrich 2006; Boyd 2013]. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §4 --> Secondary metrics are AUROC; precision at recall 0.10, 0.25 and 0.50; AP with hard negatives only; macro per-cluster AP; AP without the largest component and without the 10 largest clusters; AP in essentiality strata; and precision, recall and F1 at three pre-specified rules. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §5 --> Rule (b), the primary operating point, is the threshold maximising F1 over all hu.MAP training pairs; rule (c) predicts the top K = round(π_train × N) pairs; rule (a) is z ≥ 3 for zavg_BEF only and is non-blind (D1). <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §5.4 --> Test clusters are connected components of the test-positive graph, with components > 40 proteins split by Louvain (none needed splitting), and singleton clusters for proteins without positives. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §5.2; TR: results.itt.cluster_stats.components_split --> [Blondel 2008]

*Calibration.* Each method's scores were mapped to probabilities by isotonic regression (primary) and Platt scaling (secondary) fitted on all 634,779 hu.MAP training pairs and applied unchanged [Zadrozny & Elkan 2002; Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana 2005]. Brier score, Brier skill (against the train prevalence), calibration-in-the-large, calibration intercept and slope, ECE and reliability diagrams with log-spaced bins were reported [Van Calster 2019]. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §5.5 -->

*Uncertainty.* The primary interval is Part 1's paired cluster bootstrap: per replicate, cluster multiplicities m = bincount(integers(0, C, C)) with `numpy.random.default_rng(0)`, a within-cluster pair weighted m_c and a between-cluster pair m_c1·m_c2; B = 2,000; 95% percentile intervals; two-sided p = 2·min(P(Δ ≤ 0), P(Δ ≥ 0)) [Field & Welsh 2007]. Protein (node) and stratified pair bootstraps are secondary, the latter anti-conservative; Monte-Carlo seeds 1–4 show seed dependence [Owen 2007]. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §6 --> Thresholds and calibrators were not refitted within replicates, so their sampling error is not propagated.

*Decision rule.* zavg_BEF "clearly beats" the baselines if, against each of GLS, Spearman and Pearson, the relative AP gain is ≥ 10% (point estimate) and macro per-cluster AP and AP without the largest component are ≥ 0.95× the baseline's (intersection–union), computed by Part 1's unchanged `final_eval.decide` on S1. B was confirmatory only if zavg_BEF passed (fixed sequence). The CI-excludes-0 statistic, Holm-adjusted p-values and non-degradation ratio CIs were pre-specified and reported but do not alter the decision. zavg_BEF was to remain the Part 2 method whatever the outcome, and outcome readings were pre-written. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §7 -->

*Single-look procedure.* The freeze marker hashed the protocol, fitted rules, evaluation code and tests, package list and test files; every test-touching entry point verified it. The look required a committed intent, committed a "started" record before reading labels, and refused if any earlier look existed on disk, in any ref or in the reflog; any re-run would be automatically non-confirmatory. Commit hashes and the SHA-256 of the results were reported to the user outside the agents' reach. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §8.2–8.4; RESEARCH_TREE.md P1 --> These measures are procedural: a process with file access could read the labels, so the guarantee rests on the agent briefs, the disclosure of all prior contact and the externally reported hashes. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §8.4 "Residual limits" -->

*Verification.* Verifier A re-implemented the protocol without importing the evaluation package; Verifier B re-scored a 1% sample (seed 7 instead of the protocol's seed 0, as briefed; disclosed deviation). <!-- results/test_eval/verify/README.md, "Deviation from §8.3" --> Before the freeze, a dry run reproduced Part 1's IFT look to ≤ 1.5 × 10⁻⁸ and 34 unit tests passed. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §10 -->

### Complex construction

*Scores.* zavg_BEF was recomputed on A with the locked configuration, null moments recomputed per run by the locked procedure; on all U lines the recomputation reproduces Part 1 scores to ≤ 1e-12. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3 "Scores"; RESEARCH_TREE.md "Construction infrastructure" --> Score blocks were computed on A, on 20 PatientID-blocked 80% subsamples (seeds 1000–1019) and on 4 lineage-stratified split halves (seeds 2000–2003). <!-- results/part2/construction/README.md "Pipeline" -->

*Gene set S.* Genes with effect < −0.5 in ≥ 1% of A lines, non-missing in ≥ 95%, and not low-coverage in Part 1 (6,022 genes). <!-- zavg_map/parameters.json: S -->

*Graph.* An edge joins genes whose score is ≥ z0 and that are mutual top-k neighbours within S; weight = min(score, z0 + 3) − z0 + 1. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3 "Graph"; zavg_map/parameters.json: weight_cap -->

*Consensus and persistence.* Co-clustering frequencies over 20 subsamples × 5 Leiden seeds were clustered with Leiden-CPM over the γ grid 0.05·√2^i up to 1.6 [Traag 2019; Traag 2011; Fortunato & Barthélemy 2007; Monti 2003; Lancichinetti & Fortunato 2012; Jeub 2018; Zheng 2021 (HiDeF)]. <!-- zavg_map/parameters.json: cluster_config.gammas, seeds --> A cluster is persistent when it recurs (Jaccard ≥ 0.75) over ≥ 3 adjacent γ values with support ≥ 0.6, where support in a subsample run is containment of ≥ 75% of its members in one run cluster (decision D1, chosen on a toy simulation before any map was built because Jaccard support lost 3–4-gene complexes). <!-- DECISIONS.md D1; parameters.json: jaccard_recur, support_min, support_mode, min_adjacent --> Candidates were placed most-persistent-first, with nested candidates kept as sub-modules, duplicates dropped and partial overlaps trimmed or resolved to the more persistent cluster (D2). <!-- DECISIONS.md D2 --> Level-1 complexes have 3–60 members and all-pairs mean score ≥ z0; sub-modules come from top-down re-clustering inside each complex at γ = 0.5 and need mean score ≥ z0 + 1. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3; parameters.json: gamma2, min_size, max_size, cohesion_delta --> On the toy simulation, the chosen rules recovered 86 of 105 planted complexes (25 of 30 of size 3–4) and all 39 planted sub-modules, and returned no cluster on null data. <!-- DECISIONS.md "Toy results" (V2_containment_nested) -->

*Selection.* For each (z0, k) ∈ {4, 5, 6} × {10, 20}, each half of four PatientID-blocked lineage-stratified splits was clustered through the same pipeline; non-locus half-1 clusters of size 5–30 count as recovered when a half-2 cluster matches with Jaccard ≥ 0.5, and the statistic is the selective-gene coverage of recovered clusters, averaged over both directions and the four splits. The highest mean was selected (ties to higher z0, then lower k), and a degree-rewired baseline was reported. <!-- DECISIONS.md D6; selection.json: rule -->

*GLS comparator.* For each run, the GLS graph uses the same S and k and the same edge count as the zavg graph of that model set, with weights quantile-matched and cohesion applied on the rank-mapped scale, so both maps remove the same score quantile. <!-- DECISIONS.md D4 -->

*Locus flag and attachment.* A cluster is a locus cluster if ≥ 50% of its members lie within 10 Mb (Ensembl coordinates) or on one chromosome arm. Genes outside S are attached to a cluster when ≥ ⌈|C|/2⌉ members score ≥ z0 with the gene and a hypergeometric null gives BH q < 0.01 over all gene × cluster tests; attachments to locus clusters, on the cluster's dominant arm or within 10 Mb of a member are flagged and cannot define H1 events. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3; DECISIONS.md D3 -->

### Tiers

T1: within-cluster BioPlex edges (293T ∪ HCT116, Entrez-mapped; ≥ 3 observed members, ≥ 2 edges) above a null replacing each observed member by a random S gene of the same log2 BioPlex degree bin and A-essentiality class (10⁴ draws), BH q < 0.05 within level. T2 (not T1): mean pairwise protein correlation among ≥ 3 measured members (A lines only, ≥ 20 lines per protein) above a size-matched null (10⁴ draws), BH q < 0.05. T3-neg: testable by either source but not T1/T2; T3-nc otherwise. <!-- DECISIONS.md D5 "Tiers"; tiers/summary.json: rules --> Clusters in T1 or T2 are called complexes and the rest co-dependency modules. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.3 "Tiers" -->

### Bridge endpoint

The unit is the level-1 cluster, including locus clusters. Within each size bin {3–4, 5–8, 9–16, 17–60}, precision is pooled over within-cluster pairs with both proteins BioPlex-observed; bins are averaged with weights given by the pooled zavg + GLS cluster-size distribution after coverage matching (0.306, 0.269, 0.306, 0.119). <!-- DECISIONS.md D5; BR: primary.bin_weights --> The map with more genes drops its least stable clusters until coverage matches. The CI is a protein-node bootstrap (B = 2,000, seed 5000) with clusters fixed and each pair weighted by the product of its nodes' multiplicities; "better" requires the 95% CI to exclude 0. Sensitivities (no matching; locus clusters excluded) and secondary endpoints (co-abundance, seed 5001; stability by size; pair-level AP against BioPlex; BRIDGE-L) were fixed before validation. <!-- DECISIONS.md D5 --> Map-to-map variability was added after the map freeze as the descriptive analysis DESIGN requires, without changing the frozen maps; random size-matched references used seed 7000. <!-- results/part2/construction/README.md "Map-to-map variability"; results/part2/verify/fzc_review.md §5 -->

### Complex scores and association statistics

*Complex score.* S_C(m) is the mean over the fixed members of C of CRISPRGeneEffect, each value clipped to [−3, 3]; for a given test, the context gene(s), their paralogues within C and members within ±10 Mb of a copy-number context gene are removed; S_C is missing if fewer than half the remaining members are observed. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: score_rule; DESIGN.md §2.4 --> Alternative scores (eigengene, within-line rank, expression-masked, pan-essential-referenced) and leave-one-lineage-out and leave-top-2-out robustness are reported per hit and never used for selection. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.4 -->

*Model.* S_C ~ context + covariates by ordinary least squares: library multi-hot, ScreenType3DO and ModelType for all tests; lineage fixed effects for non-lineage contexts; log mutation burden and MSIScore for mutation contexts; ploidy, WGD and TP53 status for copy-number contexts. No covariate that is a function of the context is used. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.5, §2.2 "Covariate rule" --> *Lineage-merge convention:* indicator levels (for example OncotreeLineage levels) with fewer than two lines in the analysed sample are merged into the reference level. This engine convention is not stated in DESIGN §2.5 and was noted by the independent controls review; with full lineage fixed effects instead, β changes by ≤ 10⁻³ (relative) and p by ≤ 2% (relative) in the controls. <!-- results/part2/verify/controls_review.md §2 "One convention difference", note 2 -->

*Test statistic.* The context coefficient is tested with HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors [CITATION NEEDED: MacKinnon & White 1985 / Long & Ervin 2000] and Satterthwaite (Bell–McCaffrey) degrees of freedom [CITATION NEEDED]. A (context, unit) test is evaluable with ≥ 12 context and ≥ 12 reference A lines after complete-case filtering and ≥ 2 members after removals. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: statistic --> The HC3 implementation matches statsmodels. <!-- RESEARCH_TREE.md "FZ-LB lockbox ... stats machinery" -->

*Permutation.* Screening hits (BB-adjusted q < 0.1) are re-tested by exact PatientID-blocked Freedman–Lane permutation [Winkler 2014] with 10,000 permutations, within lineage for non-lineage contexts and unrestricted for lineage contexts; the p-value is (k + 1)/(B + 1), and a generalised Pareto tail approximation [CITATION NEEDED] is used only when that value falls below 10⁻⁴. Permutation p-values re-enter and the FDR procedure is re-run. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: statistic; results/part2/verify/h3a_fdr_diagnosis.md "Fix" --> An earlier implementation applied the tail approximation whenever there were fewer than 10 exceedances with 1,000 permutations, which inflated the size of the test by 30–80% at the relevant thresholds in simulation; it was corrected to the registered rule on synthetic data, before any real association was run. <!-- results/part2/verify/h3a_fdr_diagnosis.md "Evidence" §1, "Fix" --> In simulation the Freedman–Lane test held its type-I error at 0.05. <!-- RESEARCH_TREE.md "FZ-LB ... FL type-I 0.05" -->

*Error control.* Error control is Benjamini–Bogomolov selective FDR within each direction [CITATION NEEDED: Benjamini & Bogomolov 2014], not pooled FDR (decision D6): families are contexts; a family's p-value is the Simes combination over its units [CITATION NEEDED: Simes 1986]; families are selected by BH at q over the M families [Benjamini & Hochberg 1995]; within each of the R selected families BH is applied at q·R/M. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: fdr_rule; RESEARCH_TREE.md D6 --> This controls the expected average, over selected families, of the within-family false discovery proportion. In 1,000 simulations with planted lineage effects, the BB error rate was 0.046 ± 0.002 at q = 0.05, while the pooled FDP across all discoveries was 0.068 ± 0.002; the pooled figure is reported but not claimed. Under the global null, the probability of any discovery was 0.020 (H3a) and 0.023 (H3b). <!-- results/part2/verify/h3a_fdr_diagnosis.md "Validation" --> Sub-modules are tested stage-wise only within complexes that pass [Van den Berge 2017]. <!-- DESIGN.md §2.5 "FDR" --> Holm's procedure [CITATION NEEDED: Holm 1979] was used only for the three primary ΔAP comparisons of the test evaluation.

*Calibration and negative controls.* Per direction, 100 pseudo-contexts matched to real contexts in size and lineage composition (seed 4600), random gene sets matched for size, essentiality and coherence, and dispersed never-expressed sets are run through the same machinery; if the fraction of pseudo-context tests with p < 0.05 exceeds 0.075, every real test with p < 0.01 is re-tested by permutation. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: calibration --> [[RESULTS: calibration outcome]]

*Lockbox confirmation.* A discovery is confirmed if its effect in L has the same sign and one-sided p < 0.05 (same score, removals, covariates and test; exact permutation when L has fewer than 20 context lines), for contexts with ≥ 5 context and ≥ 5 reference L lines. Rarer contexts take a labelled rare-context route (leave-one-lineage-out, DEMETER2 on A lines and leave-one-line-out, all same sign). L effects with 95% CIs are reported for every discovery, confirmed or not. Findings are ranked as strongest only if discovery q < 0.05, lockbox-confirmed (or rare-context route), robust to leaving out each lineage and not a locus cluster. <!-- docs/registry/H3a_lineage_atlas.yaml: lockbox, success_criterion; DESIGN.md §2.5 -->

*Registry and trial ledger.* Every analysis is a committed, machine-readable registry entry (29 at FZ-R plus H1S); the association engine refuses unregistered, modified or placeholder entries and appends every run to a trial ledger. <!-- docs/registry/README.md; DESIGN.md F5 --> Freeze points (FZ-P1 protocol, FZ-LB lockbox, FZ-C maps, FZ-X contexts and families) are commits with SHA-256 manifests that the engine verifies. <!-- DESIGN.md F4 --> At FZ-X, H3a has M = 23 families and m = 8,441 tests, H3b M = 20 and m = 7,340. <!-- results/part2/fzx/README.md -->

## Limitations (draft)

1. **The single-look guarantee is procedural.** The test labels were plain files and label-free genome-wide scores covering the test pairs existed; incident D1 shows that the risk was real. One operating point is non-blind as a result. <!-- docs/TEST_PROTOCOL.md §0, §8.4 -->
2. **The gain is carried by pair-rich complexes.** Macro per-cluster AP does not differ from GLS; the per-size breakdown is inferred, not measured. The test has 238 clusters with a Kish effective number of 21.3, so CIs are wide for Spearman and Pearson. <!-- TR: results.itt.cluster_stats -->
3. **Training-label re-measurement.** zavg_BEF was selected on hu.MAP training labels derived from Complex Portal, so agreement with physical complexes partly re-measures that selection. <!-- DESIGN.md F3 -->
4. **Calibration under prevalence shift.** Calibrated probabilities under-predict by about 11% on the test set; no recalibration was made, and the calibrators' own sampling error is not propagated. <!-- TR: results.itt.metrics.zavg_BEF.calibration.isotonic.calibration_in_the_large -->
5. **The bridge endpoint is null and one secondary endpoint favours GLS.** Any statement that the zavg map is better as a map lacks support; the locus-cluster explanation is post hoc.
6. **Selection is flat across z0** at k = 10; a different z0 could have been selected with the same evidence.
7. **Locus clusters are frequent** (28% at level 1) and only flagged, not removed; T2 is not locus-aware.
8. **Stability has no chance baseline;** the variability analysis shares subsamples with the frozen map and its random reference is a floor.
9. **Some complexes merge two complexes or are pathway modules** (for example PRC1 + PRC2), so "complex" means physically supported module.
10. **Thin control coverage:** H3a rests on two controls, the H1 pass on one multi-gene readout, and H2 has none. Five controls were not evaluable at complex level.
11. **Coverage of the map:** 2,380 of the 6,022 S genes (about 40%) are in no level-1 complex, including SMARCA2, STAG1 and KIF18A, which limits paralog-based analyses. <!-- summary.json: zavg.genes_S − zavg.genes_level1; results/part2/fzx/README.md "Controls" -->
12. **Cell-line models only;** DepMap lineages are unevenly represented, and the lockbox is smaller than A (338 lines), limiting confirmation power for rare contexts. <!-- results/part2/lockbox/manifest.json -->
13. [[RESULTS: limitations specific to H3a/H3b/H1/H2 and the lockbox]]
